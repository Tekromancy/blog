## 🛡️ Who is the Austin Cyber Alliance?

The **Austin Cyber Alliance** (operating alongside the [AI Cyber Alliance](https://aicyberalliance.org)) is an independent community and defense collective dedicated to advancing cyber resilience, open-source security tooling, and hands-on offensive/defensive tradecraft across Central Texas and the broader Silicon Hills ecosystem.

As threat vectors shift toward automated offensive capabilities, kernel-level rootkits, and autonomous AI exploitation, traditional checklist compliance fails. The Alliance focuses on:

1. **Adversarial Realism**: Training through hands-on technical wargaming, attack-defense simulations, and Capture The Flag competitions.
2. **AI & Machine Learning Security**: Red-teaming neural architectures, prompt injection defenses, LLM jailbreak auditing, and automated vulnerability synthesis.
3. **Open-Source Cyber Sovereignty**: Developing and supporting open-source lab frameworks, telemetry analyzers, and sovereign computing tools.
4. **Community Collaboration**: Bridging the gap between students, seasoned industry researchers, academic institutions like ACC, and infrastructure engineers.

To learn more about Alliance initiatives or to connect with the guild, visit the official portal at [aicyberalliance.org](https://aicyberalliance.org).

## 🚀 Tonight's Presentation: "The Crucible of Hackers"

Tonight's headline briefing is an interactive 3D spatial presentation titled **"The Crucible of Hackers: The History of CTFs, Red vs. Blue Teaming, and Open-Source Cyber Ranges"**, presented by **Joshua Edward McLaughlin Cox**.

Unlike a flat, static slide deck, this presentation is built on **impress.js** as an immersive 3D flight path across 14 interactive telemetry stations in cyberspace.

### 🛰️ Flight Path Overview
1. **Cold War Military Roots & The Red Cell**: How RAND Soviet wargames and Richard Marcinko’s US Navy Red Cell laid the foundation for adversary emulation.
2. **Operation Eligible Receiver 97**: The Pentagon’s wake-up call, where 35 military hackers compromised the Department of Defense using commercial off-the-shelf software and public scripts.
3. **The Combat Triad**: Red Team (Offense), Blue Team (Defense), and Purple Team (Continuous feedback loop and telemetry verification).
4. **DEF CON 4 (1996) to Modern Attack-Defense**: From cables taped to casino floors to multi-million-dollar global cyber battlegrounds.
5. **The Open-Source Arsenal**: Deep dive into the architecture of modern open-source cyber ranges—including **CTFd**, **Google kctf**, **MITRE CALDERA**, **RootTheBox**, and **GOAD** (Game of Active Directory).
